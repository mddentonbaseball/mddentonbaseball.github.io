Our four first-year catchers improved their blocking rate from 82.6% in early fall to 87.8% by late fall, with one rising from 86.2% to 98.0%.

Across the full group from spring to fall, blocking improved from 54.9% to 84.3%, average pop time dropped from 2.18 to 2.08 seconds, and the share of throws under 2.00 seconds rose from 4% to 18%. The spring sample consisted primarily of games and the fall sample primarily of scrimmages, and three spring catchers have since departed, so the group comparison spans two different rosters.

Originally developed in January as an R Shiny application built on proprietary charting data from every scrimmage and game, the platform was later integrated with TrackMan and our post-outing reports to create a unified catcher evaluation system. Following each game, every catcher receives an individualized report covering strikes gained and lost, framing heat maps, a log of missed calls measured in inches from the zone edge, blocking performance, and throw-by-throw pop time, exchange and accuracy. Saved games compile into a season log and framing leaderboard, and all reports export to a one-page PDF or Google Sheets.

How the framing model works

Built on
1,282 taken pitches from our fall games and scrimmages.
Inputs
How many inches the pitch was from the edge of the zone, which edge it was near (top, bottom, inner or outer), and whether the count had two strikes or three balls.
Output
The chance each take gets called a strike. Strikes above average (SLAA) is called strikes minus expected strikes. SL+ puts that on a scale where 100 is average.
Runs
Framing runs count 0.13 runs per strike. A count-weighted version gives more credit for a strike in 3-2 than in 0-0.

KLAW, our pitching app

R · Shiny · Google Sheets API · Jun 2026 to now

On a phone · 0:45

KLAW delivers each pitcher's individualized program through a mobile platform used daily by our pitchers and maintained weekly by our pitching coach and me. The staff manages programming in Google Sheets, and KLAW syncs live, so every update reaches the player with a single refresh.

  • Weekly schedule. Each day is designated as bullpen, live, light, moderate, prep or off, with pre-throw, correctional, post-throw, sprint and mound work prescribed accordingly.
  • Daily routine. The complete program with sets and repetitions, including pre-throw mobility, post-throw and post-bullpen arm care, sprint and balance work, and a next-day recovery protocol.
  • Velocity profile. Pitch-by-pitch metrics including maximum and average velocity, vertical and horizontal break, spin rate, release height, extension and vertical approach angle.
  • Long-term development. A monthly return-to-throw calendar alongside a development profile and staff assessment notes.

Designed for use in the bullpen and weight room, KLAW authenticates through a Google service account to keep roster data secure.

Post-outing reports

TrackMan · Pitch AWRE · Web app · Sep 2026 to now

Walkthrough · 1:06 · demo data

Every pitcher should understand his outing before he leaves the facility. Each TrackMan file generates an individual report sheet for every pitcher, with a column for each outing he has thrown:

  • The traditional line (IP, ERA, WHIP) alongside first-pitch strike rate, race-to-two-strikes rate, strikeout and walk rates, whiff and chase rates, and batted-ball distribution.
  • Pitch metrics by pitch type, formatted to mirror the TrackMan portal: velocity, break, spin, release height, extension and approach angle.
  • Location and movement charts, plus a pitch flight visualization that traces each pitch from release to the plate and marks the hitter's swing-decision point.

Saved outings compile into a season log for each pitcher, totaling 83 outings across 15 pitchers this fall. A live at-bat chart grades every plate appearance for contact quality, and a comprehensive stat guide documents the exact definition behind every metric. All reports export to Excel.

Transfer portal board

R · Shiny · Google Sheets API · May 2026 to now

Walkthrough · 0:50

Our transfer portal evaluation platform tracks more than 3,000 players across D1 hitters, D1 pitchers and D2/D3, assigning each a performance score and tier (elite, strong, average or developing). This allows the staff to prioritize outreach toward the most impactful targets.

The dashboard highlights the top 15 hitters and pitchers along with all committed players. Staff members can filter the board, add players or edit statistics and links, and every change is rescored and saved instantly. Any view exports to CSV, and the platform is fully mobile-responsive.

How players are scored

Compared to
Only players of the same type in the same division. D1 hitters are ranked against D1 hitters, D2 pitchers against D2 pitchers, and so on.
Scaling
Each stat is put on a 0 to 1 scale within the group: best is 1, worst is 0. For stats where lower is better, the scale is flipped.
Hitters
OPS, so power counts along with getting on base, plus K/BB and games played. If a player has no slugging numbers, AVG and OBP stand in for OPS.
Pitchers
ERA 30%, K/9 28%, BB/9 22%, H/9 20% (lower is better for all but K/9).
Minimum
20 games for hitters and 20 innings for pitchers. Anyone below that is marked "low sample" instead of scored, so a 5-inning 0.00 ERA can't top the board.
Score
25 + 65 × the weighted average, so scores run from 25 to 90. Tiers: elite 80+, strong 65–79, average 50–64, developing under 50. The weights are coaching judgment, not fitted to outcomes.

Coaching philosophy

My approach is shaped by my own development as a pitcher who had to earn every inning. I prioritize structured routines, individualized development plans and objective measurement, then translate the data into clear, actionable feedback. Every player should understand what is working, what needs refinement and why. I believe sustainable improvement comes from consistency, accountability and players who take ownership of their own development.
